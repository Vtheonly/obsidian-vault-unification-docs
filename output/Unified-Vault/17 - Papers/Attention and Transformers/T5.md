T5 (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer) reframed every NLP task as **text-in, text-out** — translation, classification, QA, summarization, and even regression all become seq2seq problems with task-specific text prefixes. The paper ran a massive systematic study of pretraining objectives, datasets, and architectures, then trained a single model on the resulting recipe. T5 set the standard for encoder-decoder LLMs and pioneered the "task prefix" convention that survives in modern instruction-tuned models. See [[Papers MOC]] for the surrounding paper collection and [[T5 and BART]] for the concept-level treatment.

## Citation

Raffel, C., Shazeer, N., Roberts, A., Lee, K., Narang, S., Matena, M., Zhou, Y., Li, W., & Liu, P. (2020). *Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer*. JMLR 21(140). arXiv:1910.10683.

## The problem being solved

By 2019, transfer learning in NLP was fragmented. [[17 - Papers/Attention and Transformers/BERT|BERT]] (encoder-only) excelled at understanding tasks but could not generate. GPT-2 (decoder-only) could generate but was awkward for classification. Encoder-decoder models like BART existed but were not systematically studied. Worse, every task had a different "head" — a classifier for sentiment, a span predictor for QA, a sequence generator for translation — making it hard to do multi-task training or zero-shot transfer.

T5 asked: what if we **frame every task as text-to-text**? Then we only need one model, one loss, one training loop, and one inference API. Translation: `translate English to German: <input>` → `<output>`. Sentiment: `sentiment: <input>` → `positive` or `negative`. QA: `question: <q> context: <c>` → `<answer>`. Summarization: `summarize: <input>` → `<summary>`.

This unification has far-reaching implications. It removes the need for task-specific heads, lets a single model handle arbitrary tasks, and — critically — lets you mix tasks in training simply by including the task prefix.

## The architecture

T5 uses the original Vaswani encoder-decoder architecture (see [[Attention Is All You Need|Attention Is All You Need]]) with a few modifications.

### Pre-norm

Instead of post-norm (`LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x))`), T5 uses pre-norm (`x + Sublayer(LayerNorm(x))`). This was an early adoption of a change that would become standard for training stability — see [[Pre-Norm vs Post-Norm]].

### Relative position bias

Instead of sinusoidal or learned absolute positional embeddings, T5 uses a **relative position bias** added to the attention scores. For each attention head, a learned scalar $b_{i-j}$ is added to the attention logit between query position $i$ and key position $j$. Positions beyond a cutoff (~128) share buckets, so the model has a smooth notion of relative position that extrapolates beyond training lengths.

This was an important step toward [[RoPE]]-style relative encodings, which would become the modern default.

### Simplified activation

T5 uses `gelu_new` (a GELU variant) in the feed-forward layer, not the SwiGLU that later became standard.

### Model sizes

| Variant      | Params | Layers | d_model | d_ff | Heads |
|--------------|--------|--------|---------|------|-------|
| Small        | 60M    | 6      | 512     | 1024 | 8     |
| Base         | 220M   | 12     | 768     | 3072 | 12    |
| Large        | 770M   | 24     | 1024    | 4096 | 16    |
| 3B           | 2.8B   | 24     | 1024    | 16384| 32    |
| 11B          | 11B    | 24     | 1024    | 65536| 32    |

The 11B model used a sparse feed-forward layer (only certain experts active) to keep compute manageable — an early hint at [[Mixture of Experts]].

## The pretraining objective: span corruption

The paper systematically compared many pretraining objectives:

- Causal LM (GPT-style)
- Masked LM (BERT-style)
- Prefix LM (a hybrid)
- Span corruption (BART-style)
- Replace-corrupted spans with sentinel tokens

The winner was **span corruption**: mask contiguous spans of tokens, replace each with a single unique sentinel token (`<extra_id_0>`, `<extra_id_1>`, ...), and train the decoder to output the masked spans separated by sentinels.

Example:

```text
Input:  The quick <X> jumps over <Y> dog
Target: <X> brown fox <Y> the lazy
```

This objective has several nice properties:

- **Bidirectional** encoder (sees both sides of each span).
- **Generative** decoder (must produce text).
- **Variable span lengths** (sampled from a geometric distribution) teach the model to handle multi-token reconstruction.
- **Sentinel tokens** make it unambiguous which span goes where.

The final recipe: mask 15% of tokens, with mean span length 3.

## The C4 dataset

The authors built a new pretraining corpus, **C4 (Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus)**:

- ~750GB of cleaned English text from Common Crawl (April 2019).
- Filters: remove non-English, remove boilerplate (menus, code), deduplicate line-by-line, remove documents with bad punctuation/length patterns.
- ~156B tokens after filtering.

C4 became a standard pretraining dataset for years, used by T5, LaMDA, and many subsequent models.

## Multi-task training

The paper's most ambitious experiment was a **mixture-of-tasks** pretraining: mix span corruption with downstream tasks (translation, QA, summarization) directly in pretraining. Each example has a task prefix, and the model sees a stream of mixed-task examples.

The result: a single model that can perform all the mixed-in tasks at zero-shot inference, just by using the appropriate task prefix. This was a precursor to modern instruction-tuned LLMs.

## Key results

T5 achieved SOTA on GLUE, SuperGLUE, SQuAD, and CNN/DailyMail summarization at release time. The 11B model reached 89.3 on SuperGLUE, exceeding the previous SOTA by ~4 points and approaching human-level performance (which is ~89.8 on SuperGLUE).

The smaller variants also performed well, making T5 a popular choice for fine-tuning on task-specific data — especially for seq2seq tasks (summarization, translation) where decoder-only models are awkward.

## Why it worked

### The text-to-text framing

Framing every task as text generation is a powerful abstraction. It removes architectural differences between tasks, enables multi-task training trivially, and provides a uniform API. The convention was inherited by all modern instruction-tuned LLMs — even decoder-only models like Llama-3 use task prefixes.

### Span corruption as a generative-yet-bidirectional objective

BART had introduced denoising seq2seq, but T5's systematic comparison showed span corruption with sentinels was the best variant. It gave the encoder bidirectional context *and* trained a generative decoder — useful for both understanding and generation.

### Relative position bias

This was an important architectural improvement over absolute positional embeddings. It gave T5 modest length extrapolation (could handle longer inputs than it saw in training) and set the stage for RoPE.

### Clean pretraining data

C4's careful filtering removed a lot of the noise that hurt earlier Common Crawl-based models. The paper showed that *data quality* mattered as much as *data quantity* — a finding that [[Scaling Laws (Kaplan and Chinchilla)|Chinchilla]] later reinforced.

## Limitations

- **Encoder-decoder is parameter-inefficient**. For a given parameter budget, decoder-only models generally match or beat encoder-decoder on most tasks. By 2022, the field largely converged on decoder-only.
- **Span corruption is not natural language**. Sentinels never appear at inference time, creating a train/test mismatch. Modern instruction tuning replaced span corruption entirely.
- **No instruction tuning**. T5's prefix mechanism was the closest thing to instruction tuning at the time, but it lacked the conversational alignment that [[InstructGPT]] (and subsequent production chat assistants like ChatGPT, late 2022) would later establish.
- **2048-token context**. Same limit as GPT-3. Modern long-context models reach 100k–1M tokens.

## Successors

- **T5-v1.1**: improved pretraining recipe, released by Google.
- **mT5**: multilingual T5 pretrained on 101 languages.
- **Flan-T5** (2022): instruction-tuned T5 — performed much better on zero-shot tasks. See [[Instruction Tuning and Chat Templates]].
- **CodeT5**: variant for code understanding/generation.
- **UL2** (2022): generalized span corruption to a mixture of denoising objectives.

## Impact and legacy

T5's contributions outlasted the model itself:

1. **Text-to-text framing as default**. Every instruction-tuned LLM in 2024+ is essentially a text-to-text model. The task prefix survives in prompts like `Translate to French: ...` or `Summarize: ...`.
2. **Encoder-decoder as a viable LLM architecture**. While decoder-only dominates, encoder-decoder is still used for translation (MarianMT, NLLB) and dense retrieval (T5-based encoders).
3. **C4 as a benchmark pretraining corpus**. Used by hundreds of subsequent papers as a controlled pretraining dataset.
4. **Systematic study of pretraining choices**. The paper's ablations on objectives, datasets, and architectures set a precedent: large-scale systematic studies are valuable, not just headline SOTA results.

For practitioners in 2024–2026, Flan-T5 is still a popular open-weights model for seq2seq tasks where decoder-only models are overkill — summarization, translation, classification with structured outputs.
