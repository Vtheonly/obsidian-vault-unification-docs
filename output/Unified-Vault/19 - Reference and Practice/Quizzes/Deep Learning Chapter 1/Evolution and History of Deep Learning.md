This quiz bank practices the evolution and history of deep learning, from the 1943 McCulloch-Pitts neuron and the 1958 Perceptron to the Transformer era and the 2018 Turing Award. Its thirty questions cover the XOR critique that triggered the first AI winter, backpropagation's 1986 revival, the second winter of slow computers and small datasets, the three pillars behind the renaissance, AlexNet's 2012 ImageNet victory, GANs, and the architecture that revolutionized NLP from 2017 onward. The story is developed in [[History and Evolution of AI]], with the technical chapters in [[Perceptrons and Multi-Layer Perceptrons]], [[Backpropagation]], and [[The Transformer]]; decide on an answer before unfolding it.

## True or false

Ten statements open the quiz: three on the early decades and the first AI winter, two on the 1986 revival and the second winter, and five on the modern era from AlexNet to the Turing Award. The false statements swap inventors, exaggerate the Perceptron's power, or erase the data-and-compute half of the story — names and dates carry most of the weight here.

### Origins and the first AI winter

Three statements on the Perceptron's invention, the 1969 XOR proof, and what actually caused the first winter.

> [!question] The Perceptron was invented by Frank Rosenblatt in 1958.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> True

> [!question] The first AI Winter was caused by the discovery that the Perceptron could solve any problem.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> False

> [!question] Minsky and Papert proved in 1969 that a single-layer Perceptron cannot solve XOR.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> True

### Revival and the second winter

Two statements on the algorithm that revived neural networks and what dominated the field from the 1990s to 2006.

>> True

> [!question] During the second AI Winter (1990s-2006), neural networks were the dominant AI approach.

## Multiple choice

Ten questions follow the timeline: the Perceptron and its fatal limitation, the 1986 revival, the second winter's causes and its dominant algorithms, then AlexNet, GANs, the three pillars, the Transformer, and the 1943 origins. Attribution questions mix real pioneers with plausible alternatives, so match each name to its contribution precisely.

### The Perceptron and the first AI winter

Two questions on who invented the Perceptron and what problem brought the first winter on.

> [!question] Who invented the Perceptron in 1958?
> a) Geoffrey Hinton
> b) Frank Rosenblatt
> c) Yann LeCun
> d) Ian Goodfellow
>> [!success]- Answer
>> b) Frank Rosenblatt

> [!question] What problem caused the first AI Winter?
> a) Lack of funding for AI
> b) The Perceptron was proven unable to solve non-linearly separable problems like XOR
> c) GPUs were not invented yet
> d) The internet did not exist
>> [!success]- Answer
>> b) The Perceptron was proven unable to solve non-linearly separable problems like XOR

### Backpropagation and the second winter

Two questions on the 1986 algorithm and why the field stalled again afterward.

> [!question] What algorithm, popularized in 1986, allowed multi-layer networks to learn?
> a) Random Forest
> b) Support Vector Machine
> c) Backpropagation
> d) K-Means
>> [!success]- Answer
>> c) Backpropagation

> [!question] Why did the second AI Winter (1990s-2006) occur?
> a) Backpropagation was mathematically wrong
> b) Computers were too slow and datasets too small for deep networks
> c) Neural networks were banned
> d) SVMs were proven to be always superior
>> [!success]- Answer
>> b) Computers were too slow and datasets too small for deep networks

### The deep learning renaissance

Four questions on AlexNet's innovation, GANs' inventor, the three pillars, and the architecture that revolutionized NLP.

> [!question] What was the key innovation of AlexNet in 2012?
> a) It used CPUs instead of GPUs
> b) It was a very shallow network
> c) It used GPUs and deep CNNs to crush the ImageNet competition
> d) It did not use any training data
>> [!success]- Answer
>> c) It used GPUs and deep CNNs to crush the ImageNet competition

> [!question] Who invented GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks)?
> a) Geoffrey Hinton
> b) Yann LeCun
> c) Ian Goodfellow
> d) Yoshua Bengio
>> [!success]- Answer
>> c) Ian Goodfellow

> [!question] What are the three pillars that enabled modern Deep Learning?
> a) Big Data, Compute (GPUs), and Algorithms
> b) Big Data, CPUs, and Python
> c) Algorithms, Java, and Cloud
> d) Data, Math, and Philosophy
>> [!success]- Answer
>> a) Big Data, Compute (GPUs), and Algorithms

> [!question] What architecture revolutionized NLP starting in 2017?
> a) CNN
> b) RNN
> c) Transformer
> d) Perceptron
>> [!success]- Answer
>> c) Transformer

### Origins and the winter's winners

Two questions flanking the timeline: the 1943 mathematical neuron, and the algorithms that dominated while neural networks languished.

> [!question] What did McCulloch and Pitts create in 1943?
> a) The first GPU
> b) The first mathematical model of an artificial neuron
> c) The first programming language
> d) The first dataset
>> [!success]- Answer
>> b) The first mathematical model of an artificial neuron

> [!question] Which algorithms dominated during the second AI Winter?
> a) Deep Neural Networks
> b) Transformers and GANs
> c) SVMs and Random Forests
> d) Reinforcement Learning
>> [!success]- Answer
>> c) SVMs and Random Forests
